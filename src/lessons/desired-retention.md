title: Desired retention and what it costs
group: scheduling
evidence: fsrs
summary: How much to remember, and why 95% costs far more than 90%.

Desired retention is the chance you still remember a card when it comes up. At 90% you forget about one card in ten when it is due. Every preset sets one.

## Higher costs more, fast

FSRS schedules a card for the day its recall chance falls to your target. A higher target means shorter gaps, and the extra reviews grow quickly near the top. Going from 90% to 95% roughly doubles the reviews. Going to 97% or 99% costs far more again, for very little extra memory.

A lower target means longer gaps and fewer reviews. Below about 80% you forget so often that relearning eats the savings.

## Picking a number

- 90% suits most material. It is AiNKI's standard.
- 95% is for an exam you must pass. Use it for weeks, not years.
- 85% is a good choice when time is short and the material matters less.
- 80% keeps old knowledge around with very little work.

For many people the least total work lies somewhere between 80% and 90%. It differs from person to person, so AiNKI does not guess. Open a preset and look at What it costs: it simulates the next 30, 90 or 365 days with your own memory and your own speed, at several targets.

## Did you reach it?

Stats show how much of each deck you really remembered over the last 30 days next to its aim. A few points either way is normal. If you are well below, try fewer new cards for a while, or [optimize FSRS](/learn/optimizing).
