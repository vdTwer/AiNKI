title: Feeling you know it
group: memory
evidence: moderate
summary: Familiar isn't remembered. How your sense of knowing can mislead you.

Rereading notes feels productive. The text becomes familiar, and familiar feels like known. But when the notes are gone, much of it is not there. Researchers call this an illusion of competence.

## What the research says

Students often rate rereading and highlighting as their best strategies. In studies these are among the least effective. People also expect to remember better after cramming than after spacing, and the reverse is true. Our sense of how well we know something is a poor guide on its own.

## What helps

- Test, don't look. Try to recall before you show the answer.
- Be strict. If you recognized the answer only after seeing it, press Again.
- Check your sense of knowing. Turn on the sure check in settings. Before each answer AiNKI asks whether you are sure. Stats then show how often you were right when you felt sure, and when you didn't. It does not change your schedule; it shows how far to trust the feeling.
