title: Two buttons or four
group: scheduling
evidence: fsrs
summary: Again and Good are enough. If you use Hard, use it only for cards you did remember.

AiNKI shows two buttons by default: Again when you forgot, Good when you remembered. You can turn on Hard and Easy in settings.

## Two is enough

FSRS learns most from one question: did you remember or not? With two buttons the answer is always clear, and grading is fast. FSRS works well this way.

Four buttons add a little information when they are used consistently. Easy says the card was trivial; FSRS then waits longer. Hard says you got there with effort.

## The one mistake to avoid

Hard means you remembered. If you did not remember the card, press Again, even if you almost had it. Pressing Hard for a forgotten card tells FSRS you recalled it. It then schedules the card too far out, and the optimizer learns from wrong data.

A simple rule: if you had to look at the answer to know it, it is Again.

## Speed doesn't count

AiNKI never turns a slow answer into Hard or a fast one into Easy. How long you thought is recorded, but research on response times found them too noisy to grade by. The only exception is a time limit you set yourself: when time runs out the card counts as forgotten. See [timed and typed answers](/learn/timed-and-typed).
