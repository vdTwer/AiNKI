title: Lists and multicards
group: cards
evidence: practice
summary: Lists are hard to learn as one card. Split them, or let AiNKI track each item.

Lists are among the hardest things to memorize. "The seven wonders of the ancient world" fails whenever you miss one wonder, so the card keeps coming back, and you relearn six items you already knew.

## Three ways to learn a list

- Split it into one card per item, each with a question that points to one answer. This works best when the items mean something on their own.
- Make it a multicard. AiNKI then keeps a separate memory for every item on the back. When you study, you tap the ones you remembered. Items you know well rest; only the ones you missed come back soon. The card still shows the whole list, so you learn it as a set.
- Ask about the order or the structure instead, when that is what matters. "What comes after Gemini?" is easier to learn than all twelve signs at once.

## When AiNKI suggests a split

The coach flags a card that asks a lot at once: a list on the back that you keep forgetting, or a card you answer slowly and often miss. Its split page lets you turn the items into separate cards, with or without keeping the original.
