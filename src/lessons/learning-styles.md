title: Learning styles
group: memory
evidence: against
summary: Teaching to "visual" or "auditory" learners doesn't improve learning.

Many people believe they are visual, auditory or hands-on learners, and learn best when taught that way. The idea is popular in schools and books. It has been tested, and it does not hold up.

## What the research says

To support learning styles, a study would need to show that visual learners learn better with pictures and auditory learners learn better by listening. A careful review in 2008 found almost no studies with that design, and the few that existed did not find the effect. Later studies agree.

People do have preferences. Following them doesn't make learning better.

## What does matter

- The material decides the form. Maps are learned from maps, pronunciation from sound.
- Pictures help almost everyone remember. See [images and mnemonics](/learn/images-and-mnemonics).
- [Spacing](/learn/spacing) and [testing yourself](/learn/testing) work for everyone.

## Sources

- Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008). Learning styles: Concepts and evidence. Psychological Science in the Public Interest.
- Husmann, P. R., & O'Loughlin, V. D. (2019). Another nail in the coffin for learning styles? Anatomical Sciences Education.
