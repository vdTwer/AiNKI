title: Timed and typed answers
group: cards
evidence: mixed
summary: Typing makes you commit to an answer. A time limit trains speed, not memory.

Presets can ask you to type the answer before it is shown, and can set a time limit. Both are off by default. Both apply only to cards you have seen before.

## Typed answers

Producing an answer yourself is remembered better than reading it. This is the generation effect, and it is well documented. Typing also makes you commit. You can't tell yourself you "sort of knew it" when your answer is on the screen next to the right one. AiNKI shows what you wrote above the answer; you still decide whether you remembered.

The cost is time. Typing is slower than thinking the answer. It suits short answers such as vocabulary.

## Time limits

There is little research on time limits for flashcards. Time pressure can make recall harder and doesn't make memories stronger. A limit is useful when speed is the goal: words you need in a conversation, facts for a timed exam.

When time runs out, the card counts as forgotten. You can add 10 seconds once per card. AiNKI does not use answer times for anything else. A slow correct answer is still a correct answer.

## Sources

- Bertsch, S., Pesta, B. J., Wiscott, R., & McDaniel, M. A. (2007). The generation effect: A meta-analytic review. Memory & Cognition.
