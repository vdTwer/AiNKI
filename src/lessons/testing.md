title: Testing yourself beats rereading
group: memory
evidence: strong
summary: Pulling an answer from memory strengthens it more than looking at it again.

When you try to recall something, the effort itself makes the memory stronger. Reading the answer again does much less. This is called the testing effect, or retrieval practice.

## What the research says

In a well known study, students read a short text. Some reread it, others wrote down what they remembered. After five minutes the rereaders did a little better. After a week the students who had tested themselves remembered far more. Meta-analyses of many such studies find a moderate to large benefit, in classrooms as well as labs.

It works even when you fail to recall, as long as you see the answer afterwards.

## For you

- Really try before you show the answer. Give the answer in your head, or out loud, or type it.
- Don't press Good because the answer looks familiar. Recognizing is not recalling.
- Flashcards are retrieval practice by design. That is why they work.
